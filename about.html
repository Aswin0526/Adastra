About Us

Sometimes the journey teaches lot about your destination.

Ad Astra Is a Student-Run, Multidisciplinary Team Driven to Design and Build the Next Generation of Mars Rovers to Explore the Red Planet's Habitability. Students from various disciplines and technical clubs from our institution were shortlisted and handpicked based on their interest and skillset. Thus, our team comprises of 71 space enthusiasts with inquisitive minds who want to learn and work with extraterrestrial robotics in preparation for upcoming human exploration of Mars. We connect with the public digitally via our social media handles and our institution’s landing pages.

VISION

Our vision is to inspire and cultivate the next generation of space explorers and scientists by creating a comprehensive and accessible program for students to engage in cutting-edge research and development in the field of space technology. We aim to foster a community of lifelong learners and innovators who will shape the future of space exploration.

MISSION

The mission of the student rover team is to design, build, and operate a rover capable of exploring and conducting scientific experiments on other planets while inspiring and educating future generations of scientists, engineers, and space enthusiasts.

Mechanical Design

Engineering the Future of Martian Exploration with Precision and Durability

The Mechanical Subsystem designs and constructs a robust rover structure to endure Mars’ harsh terrain. The chassis is engineered for optimal strength, shock absorption, and weight distribution, ensuring stability across rough environments. The 6 Degrees of Freedom (DOF) Robotic Arm features precision actuators for intricate tasks like sample collection and scientific operations, offering flexibility and control. A specialized gripper ensures secure handling of objects with high-torque actuators, even under extreme conditions. The rover’s wheel design is optimized for traction on rocky and uneven surfaces, with independent wheel movement for enhanced maneuverability. The system is modular for easy maintenance and upgrades, ensuring long-term reliability. With strict adherence to engineering standards, the Mechanical Subsystem is crucial to the rover’s success in Martian exploration.

Software Subsystem:

Enabling Intelligent Autonomy and Seamless Operations

The Software Subsystem of Team Ad Astra’s Nikola V3 rover ensures intelligent, autonomous operations across all systems. Running on ROS 2 (Robot Operating System), it provides modular control and real-time management for tasks such as sensor fusion, path planning, and autonomous navigation. The software integrates AI algorithms for real-time image processing, object detection, and object tracking to identify specimens and perform life-existence tests. It also manages communication protocols, enabling seamless data exchange between rover components and the control station. Powered by NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin with Ubuntu (JetPack), the subsystem supports deep learning tasks and high-performance processing for decision-making and environmental interaction. Through this robust software framework, the rover can navigate and adapt autonomously in challenging Martian terrain, ensuring mission success.

Astrobiology Subsystem:

Exploring Mars' Potential for Life

The Astrobiology Subsystem of Team Ad Astra’s Nikola V3 rover integrates advanced sensors for comprehensive environmental and soil analysis, crucial for astrobiological research. Atmospheric sensors measure temperature, pressure, humidity, air quality, hydrogen, and carbon monoxide to monitor Martian conditions. Soil analysis is conducted using electrochemical sensors to assess pH, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium levels, while capacitive moisture and thermal infrared sensors evaluate soil moisture and temperature. Gas sensors detect atmospheric gases and pollutants, while miniaturized spectrometers analyze chemical compositions. The rover’s customized drill system collects soil samples from various depths, ensuring sample integrity. The Ninhydrin test detects amino acids, offering insights into potential biological activity. Spectrometric and chemical analysis further identify organic molecules, providing valuable data on Mars' astrobiological potential.

Electrical Subsystem:

Enabling Intelligent Power, Control, and Precision for Exploration

The Electrical Subsystem of Team Ad Astra’s Nikola V3 rover ensures seamless power management and precise control over the rover’s systems. At its core, the NVIDIA AGX Jetson Orin drives autonomous decision-making, while the AtMega 2560 microcontroller manages high-speed processing and actuator control. Power distribution is optimized through multiple buck converters, maintaining stable voltage to critical components. The Vecmocon 12V 50Ah LiFePO4 battery provides a robust, long-lasting energy source, designed for reliability and safety in extreme conditions. Solid-state relays ensure efficient power switching, while current sensors deliver real-time monitoring of electrical consumption, enabling precise diagnostics and optimal system performance. This integrated power and control architecture guarantees robust electrical functionality, supporting the rover’s demanding missions on Mars.

Control Subsystem:

Enabling Precision, Autonomy, and Innovation in Exploration

The Control Subsystem of Team Ad Astra's Nikola V3 rover integrates advanced hardware and software to ensure precise mobility, actuator control, and science lab management. PID control algorithms regulate rover speed and direction, enabling smooth and accurate movement. The robotic arm is controlled using PI feedback systems, ensuring precise sample collection and manipulation. The Atmega 2560 microcontroller, running on FreeRTOS, handles low-level control with real-time scheduling for efficient operations. Reliable communication is achieved via serial communication and 5GHz radio, ensuring seamless data exchange with the control station. Path planning and obstacle avoidance algorithms enable the rover to navigate challenging terrains autonomously, ensuring efficient exploration and data gathering.
